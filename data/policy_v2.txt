# University Merit Scholarship and Financial Assistance Policy
Document Code: POL-FA-2024-V2
Effective Date: August 1, 2024

Section 1: Purpose and Scope
This policy establishes the institutional guidelines and regulations governing undergraduate merit scholarships and financial assistance programs administered by the University Financial Aid Committee. It applies to all matriculated full-time undergraduate students enrolled across all faculties.

Section 2: Eligibility Criteria
To qualify for the University Merit Scholarship, an applicant must be a citizen or permanent resident enrolled in an accredited degree program. Candidates are required to hold and preserve an overall CGPA of at least 7.5 on the university 10.0 scale. Applicants must be no older than 26 years of age at the time of initial matriculation. Any applicant with an active disciplinary sanction is ineligible.

Section 3: Financial Award Limits
The maximum annual scholarship award shall not exceed $6,500 per academic calendar year. Funding covers tuition assistance up to 80% of total tuition fees assessed by the registrar. Laboratory, hostel, and miscellaneous student activity fees are non-reimbursable and remain the sole responsibility of the recipient.

Section 5: Minimum Credit Load
Recipients are required to maintain full-time academic status by enrolling in at least 14 credit hours each semester. Dropping below this credit requirement without prior formal authorization from the Academic Advisor will trigger immediate suspension of financial benefits.

Section 4: Application Deadlines
All candidate applications must be formally submitted through the financial portal by June 15, 2024. A late submission window is permissible until June 30, 2024, subject to an administrative late fee of $75. Incomplete or late submissions past this date will not be reviewed under any circumstances.

Section 6: Appeals and Grievance Procedure
Any student whose scholarship application or renewal is declined may submit a written appeal within 14 days of official notification. Appeals must be directed to the Office of the Dean of Student Affairs along with supporting academic records or extenuating hardship documentation. Decisions rendered by the Appeals Board are final and binding.

Section 7: Renewal and Continuation
Scholarship continuation is reviewed annually at the conclusion of the academic year. To retain eligibility for continuing support, scholars must file a timely renewal request, maintain a cumulative CGPA of 7.5, and record a minimum lecture attendance rate of 85% across all enrolled courses.

Section 8: Disqualification and Penalties
A recipient whose academic performance declines below a cumulative CGPA of 6.8 shall be placed on formal financial probation for one subsequent semester. If academic progress is not restored, the scholarship award shall be terminated, and the student may be required to reimburse disbursed funds with a penalty interest of 8%.

Section 9: Disbursement Schedule
Financial aid disbursements are released in two equal installments directly toward the student tuition account. The initial disbursement will take place on September 25, and the second disbursement will occur on February 5, following institutional registration confirmation.

Section 10: General Inquiries and Contact
For questions regarding award status, documentation submission, or financial counseling, students should contact the Office of Student Financial Services via email at aid@university.edu or visit Student Services Building, Room 204.
